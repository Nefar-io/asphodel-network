CONTAINMENT DOCTRINE
INTERNAL RESPONSE POLICY — ASPHODEL CONTINUITY DIRECTIVE — REF: ASP-CD-001
This document establishes ASPHODEL's operational philosophy regarding secrecy breaches, asset compromise, and organizational continuity threats. It is not a field manual. It is the framework within which all field decisions are made. Personnel operating under ASPHODEL authority are expected to understand and internalize this doctrine — not reference it during operations.

The objective of every action taken under this doctrine is silence. Not destruction. Not punishment. Silence.
// SECTION 1 — FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLEASP-CD-001-A
ASPHODEL exists because some situations cannot be resolved through normal ETTRA channels without creating more exposure than the original problem. The core purpose of this organization is not to act — it is to make sure certain things don't need to be acted on again. Prevention is the primary function. Intervention is the failure mode of prevention.
This distinction matters. An organization oriented around intervention will manufacture situations that require intervention. An organization oriented around prevention will work to eliminate those situations before they develop. ASPHODEL is, by doctrine, a prevention organization that retains intervention capability as a last resort — not as a first response.
// INTERNAL NOTE — HICOM GUIDANCE
Personnel who join ASPHODEL expecting regular deployment are misunderstanding the organization's function. Successful operation looks like nothing happening. If you are frequently deployed, something has already gone wrong further up the chain.
// SECTION 2 — INFORMATION SUPPRESSIONASP-CD-001-B
The most reliable form of containment is suppression at the source. Before an information breach spreads, it has a point of origin. Identifying and neutralizing that origin — through operational means, not physical ones — is the first priority when a breach is detected.
Suppression methods are applied in sequence. Each step is authorized only after the previous has been attempted and assessed as insufficient. Personnel are not authorized to skip steps based on personal judgment about urgency. Urgency does not override procedure. If the situation is genuinely too urgent for procedure, it should have been escalated earlier.
  • 01.Channel isolation — remove compromised communication vectors from operational structure. No confrontation. No notification to the source.
  • 02.Content removal — systematic elimination of flagged material from accessible archives. Document what was removed, not why.
  • 03.Source monitoring — passive surveillance of the identified origin point. Confirm scope before action.
  • 04.Source isolation — remove the identified origin from operational access without confrontation or explanation.
  • 05.Formal review — escalate to HICOM with documented evidence. Do not act beyond isolation without formal authorization.
    • // step 4 is where people start improvising. that's always where it goes sideways.
    // SECTION 3 — DISCREDITATION & NARRATIVE CONTROLASP-CD-001-C
    When suppression at the source is not fully achievable, narrative control becomes the secondary objective. Information that cannot be eliminated can often be rendered ineffective — if the source loses credibility, the information loses impact. This is not about dishonesty as a value. It is about operational pragmatism.
    Discreditation is applied carefully and specifically. The objective is to reduce the operational impact of a breach, not to destroy an individual. Personnel applying discreditation measures are expected to maintain this distinction. Excessive action is itself a form of exposure — it draws attention to the situation being managed.
    // OPERATOR ANNOTATION
    The line between "neutralizing a threat's credibility" and "just making someone look bad for personal reasons" is not always obvious under operational pressure. If you're not sure which side of that line you're on, stop and request guidance. The review process is less damaging than the alternative.
    // RESTRICTION NOTICE
    Discreditation measures may only be applied against individuals whose operational threat has been assessed and documented. Informal discreditation — not formally authorized and recorded — is a violation of this doctrine and will result in review of the personnel who applied it, not just those who were subject to it.
    // SECTION 4 — EXTRACTION & OPERATIONAL ISOLATIONASP-CD-001-D
    Extraction is the removal of an asset or personnel member from their current operational context — not from the organization, and not through force. The purpose of extraction is to create distance between a compromised or at-risk individual and the operational structure they are connected to. This protects both the individual and the structure.
    Operational isolation is a longer-term version of extraction. Where extraction is immediate and situational, isolation is sustained and deliberate. An isolated individual continues to exist within ETTRA's broader context but has no access to ASPHODEL-adjacent operations, communications, or personnel. Isolation is not a punishment. It is a firewall.
  • 01.Access revocation — quietly remove operational access without triggering a visible change in status.
  • 02.Communication redirect — re-route any incoming operational communication away from the isolated individual.
  • 03.Social distancing — reduce inter-personnel contact through scheduling and routing adjustments. Organic, not enforced.
  • 04.Formal status review — once isolation is confirmed stable, initiate HICOM review of long-term disposition.
    • // "organic, not enforced" sounds simple. it's the hardest part of the whole process to get right.
    // SECTION 5 — IDENTITY MANAGEMENT & MEMORY SUPPRESSIONASP-CD-001-E
    In cases of severe operational exposure, identity management protocols may be invoked. These are not about deception — they are about separation. When an individual's identity has become operationally compromised, that identity must be separated from ASPHODEL's operational structure in every accessible record.
    Memory suppression, as a doctrine concept, refers to the systematic reduction of an individual's documented operational footprint. Logs, communication records, archive entries — anything that connects an identity to ASPHODEL operations is reviewed, reduced to minimum necessary content, and archived under restricted access. The individual is not erased. The connection is.
    // CLASSIFICATION NOTICE — ASP-CD-001-E
    Identity management and memory suppression protocols are authorized only under Level III conditions or above. Unauthorized application of these protocols to individuals without documented justification is a serious violation of ETTRA internal security policy. Authorization chain must be documented before and after application — not reconstructed retroactively.

    This section describes measures that represent the absolute end of the containment escalation chain. They are not the primary objective of ASPHODEL's doctrine — they are the final contingency after all other options have been assessed, attempted, and found insufficient. Misapplication of measures described in this section constitutes a violation of ETTRA authority regardless of operational level.
    When all containment options — suppression, isolation, extraction, identity management — have failed to adequately address an active threat to ASPHODEL or ETTRA operational security, permanent asset removal may be authorized. This is not a preferred outcome. It is the outcome that exists because some situations leave no other viable path.
    Permanent removal under this doctrine refers to the complete and irrecoverable severing of an individual from any connection to ETTRA, ASPHODEL, or related operational structures. This includes but is not limited to: permanent access termination, identity purge across all accessible records, exclusion from all future operational contact, and in extreme cases, formal external reporting to relevant authorities — removing the individual from ASPHODEL's operational environment entirely through institutional means.
